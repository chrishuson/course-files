# Context for Codex: Grade 9 Physics Relativity Worksheet

Teacher: high school math/physics teacher preparing a 9th grade physics follow-up worksheet.

Prior lesson:
- Reference frames / frames of reference.
- Michelson-Morley experiment and the failed search for the ether.
- Light speed as constant in all inertial frames.
- Lorentz length contraction: objects moving near light speed are measured shorter in the direction of motion.
- Time dilation: moving clocks run slower, explained with a light-clock diagram.
- Relativistic mass/inertia: better phrased as “rest mass stays the same, but energy/momentum/inertia increase.” Particle accelerators are a good example.

Student level:
- Grade 9 physics.
- Emphasis on conceptual questions, discussion, and manageable calculations.
- Include unit conversions and scientific notation.
- Avoid advanced algebra except substitution into formulas.

Worksheet goals:
1. Review reference frames.
2. Explain the Michelson-Morley experiment.
3. Practice unit conversions involving large speeds.
4. Compare Earth’s orbital speed with the speed of light.
5. Calculate energy from E = mc^2.
6. Interpret why tiny mass changes can release large energy.
7. Include conceptual questions on length contraction, time dilation, and particle accelerators.

Useful constants:
- Speed of light: c = 3.00 × 10^8 m/s
- Earth orbital speed around Sun: about 30 km/s
- 1 km = 1000 m
- 1 minute = 60 s
- 1 hour = 3600 s
- 1 day = 86,400 s
- 1 megaton TNT equivalent ≈ 4.184 × 10^15 J
- Proton rest energy ≈ 938 MeV
- LHC proton energy ≈ 6.5 to 7 TeV, so gamma is roughly 7000; old-style “relativistic mass” interpretation: about 7000 times the proton’s rest mass.

Potential calculation types:
- Convert 30 km/s to m/s.
- Find how far Earth travels through its orbit in one hour or one day.
- Convert speed of light from m/s to km/s.
- Find how far light travels in one second, one minute, or one year.
- Compare c to Earth orbital speed: c / 30 km/s ≈ 10,000.
- Use E = mc^2 for small masses, e.g. 1 gram, 0.001 kg.
- Compare energy from 1 g converted fully to energy with TNT equivalent.
- Compute old-style relativistic inertia increase: if gamma = 7000, a proton behaves as if it has 7000 times its rest inertia.
