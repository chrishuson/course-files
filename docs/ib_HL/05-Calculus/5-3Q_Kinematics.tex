Let $f(x) = (1 + 3x)\,e^{-2x}$.

  \item Using the Maclaurin series for $e^{x}$, write down the first four

  \item Hence find the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$, up to and including
  the term in $x^{3}$. \hfill [3]
  \item Hence write down the value of $f''(0)$. \hfill [1]
\end{enumerate}

\item[2.] [Maximum mark: 7]

A particle moves in a straight line. Its velocity, $v \text{ m s}^{-1}$, at
time $t$ seconds is given by
\[
v(t) = t^{2} - 7t + 10, \qquad 0 \leq t \leq 6.
\]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.15cm]
  \item Find the acceleration of the particle when $t = 4$. \hfill [2]
  \item Find the values of $t$ for which the particle is at rest.
  \hfill [2]
  \item Find the total distance travelled by the particle for
  $0 \leq t \leq 6$. \hfill [3]
\end{enumerate}

%--- Problem 3: Maclaurin series of ln(1 + sin x) from derivatives; limit; series approximation of an integral and percentage error [13 marks] ---
%--- Source: Original (freshly authored 2026-10-28, HL syllabus AHL 5.12, 5.13, 5.19) ---
\item[3.] [Maximum mark: 13]

Let $f(x) = \ln(1 + \sin x)$, for $-\frac{\pi}{2} < x < \frac{3\pi}{2}$.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.05cm]
  \item Show that $f'(x) = \dfrac{\cos x}{1 + \sin x}$ and
  $f''(x) = -\dfrac{1}{1 + \sin x}$. \hfill [3]
  \item Find $f'''(x)$. \hfill [2]
  \item Hence find the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$, up to and including
  the term in $x^{3}$. \hfill [3]
  \item Use your answer to part (c) to find
  $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\ln(1 + \sin x) - x}{x^{2}}$. \hfill [2]
  \item Use your answer to part (c) to find an approximate value of
  $\int_{0}^{0.4} \ln(1 + \sin x)\,\mathrm{d}x$, and find the
  percentage error in this approximation. \hfill [3]
\end{enumerate}


%--- Problem 4: Car with a = -0.02v^2: separable DE for v(t); time to slow to 12; distance by integration; v(s) from a = v dv/ds [14 marks] ---
%--- Source: Original (freshly authored 2026-10-28, HL syllabus SL 5.9, AHL 5.18) ---
\item[4.] [Maximum mark: 14]

A car's engine is switched off at time $t = 0$, when its velocity is
$30 \text{ m s}^{-1}$. For $t \geq 0$ its acceleration is $a = -0.02v^{2}$,
where $v \text{ m s}^{-1}$ is its velocity at time $t$ seconds.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.05cm]
  \item By solving a differential equation, show that
  $v = \dfrac{30}{1 + 0.6t}$. \hfill [4]
  \item Find the time at which the speed of the car is
  $12 \text{ m s}^{-1}$. \hfill [2]
  \item Find the distance travelled by the car in this time. \hfill [3]
  \item Given that $a = v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}s}$, where $s$ metres
  is the distance travelled since $t = 0$, find an expression for $v$ in
  terms of $s$. \hfill [3]
  \item Hence find the speed of the car when it has travelled
  $100$ metres. \hfill [2]
\end{enumerate}


%--- Problem 5: dy/dx = x + 2y: Maclaurin series from the DE; exact solution by integrating factor (by parts); percentage error at x = 0.2 [13 marks] ---
%--- Source: Original (freshly authored 2026-10-28, HL syllabus AHL 5.16, 5.18, 5.19) ---
\item[5.] [Maximum mark: 13]

The function $y = f(x)$ satisfies the differential equation
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = x + 2y$, and $y = 1$ when $x = 0$.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.05cm]
  \item Use the differential equation to find the values of $f'(0)$,
  $f''(0)$ and $f'''(0)$. \hfill [4]
  \item Hence find the Maclaurin series for $y$, up to and including the
  term in $x^{3}$. \hfill [2]
  \item By using an integrating factor, solve the differential equation.
  Give your answer in the form $y = f(x)$. \hfill [5]
  \item Use your series from part (b) to estimate $f(0.2)$, and find the
  percentage error in this estimate. \hfill [2]
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
