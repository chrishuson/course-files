\subsubsection*{7.4 Classwork: Solving Exponential Equations (calculator)}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4cm]

%--- Problem 1: Technique drill --- solve b^(...) = c using logs ---
\item[1.] [Maximum mark: 8]

Solve each equation for $x$. Give your answer correct to three
significant figures.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4cm]
  \item $3^x = 17$ \hfill [2]

  \vspace{3.4cm}

  \item $4^{\,x-2} = 30$ \hfill [2]

  \vspace{3.4cm}

  \item $2^{\,3x+1} = 100$ \hfill [2]

  \vspace{3.4cm}

  \item $5^{\,x} = 7^{\,x-1}$ \hfill [2]

  \vspace{3.4cm}
\end{enumerate}

%--- Problem 2: Bacteria growth (calculator-required answers) ---
\item[2.] [Maximum mark: 7]

The number of bacteria $P$ in a culture, $t$ hours after the start of
the experiment, is modelled by
$$P(t) \;=\; 200 \cdot 3^{\,t/10}.$$

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4cm]
  \item Write down the number of bacteria at the start of the experiment.
  \hfill [1]

  \vspace{2.5cm}

  \item Find the number of bacteria after $24$ hours. Give your answer
  to the nearest integer. \hfill [2]

  \vspace{4.5cm}

  \item Find the time, in hours, at which the number of bacteria first
  reaches $5000$. Give your answer correct to one decimal place.
  \hfill [4]

  \vspace{5cm}
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

%--- Problem 3: Radioactive decay (iodine-131) ---
\item[3.] [Maximum mark: 7]

Iodine-131 is a radioactive isotope used in medicine. The mass
remaining, in grams, $t$ days after a sample is prepared, is modelled by
$$M(t) \;=\; M_0 \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/T},$$
where $M_0$ is the initial mass and $T$ is the half-life. For
iodine-131, $T = 8$ days. A sample initially has $M_0 = 50$ grams.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4cm]
  \item Find the mass remaining after $20$ days. Give your answer
  correct to three significant figures. \hfill [2]

  \vspace{4.5cm}

  \item Find the time at which $60\,\%$ of the original mass has decayed.
  Give your answer correct to three significant figures. \hfill [3]

  \vspace{5cm}

  \item Find, to the nearest day, the time after which only $1$ gram of
  iodine-131 remains. \hfill [2]

  \vspace{3cm}
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

%--- Problem 4: Newton's-cooling style with explicit base 0.9 ---
\item[4.] [Maximum mark: 6]

A cup of tea is poured. Its temperature $T$, in degrees Celsius,
$t$ minutes after pouring, is modelled by
$$T(t) \;=\; 20 \;+\; 65 \cdot (0.9)^t.$$

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4cm]
  \item State the temperature of the tea at the moment it is poured.
  \hfill [1]

  \vspace{2.5cm}

  \item Find the temperature of the tea after $15$ minutes. Give your
  answer correct to three significant figures. \hfill [2]

  \vspace{4.5cm}

  \item The tea is comfortable to drink when its temperature reaches
  $40\,^{\circ}\text{C}$. Find, correct to three significant figures,
  the time at which this occurs. \hfill [3]

  \vspace{5cm}
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
