%--- Problem 1: Chain rule with e^(x^2+1) [4 marks] ---
%--- Source: AA SL 2022 Nov P1 Q02 ---
\item[1.] [Maximum mark: 4]

The function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = e^{x^2 + 1}$, where $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
Find $g'(-1)$.

\textbf{Solution:}

Differentiate using the chain rule:

$g'(x) = e^{x^2 + 1} \cdot 2x$ \hfill \textbf{(M1)(A1)}

\smallskip
Substitute $x = -1$:

$g'(-1) = e^{(-1)^2 + 1} \cdot 2(-1) = e^2 \cdot (-2)$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

$g'(-1) = -2e^2$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

\bigskip

\hrule

\bigskip

%--- Problem 2: Quotient rule with ln x / x^4 [16 marks] ---
%--- Source: AA SL 2021 May P1 TZ1 Q08 ---
\item[2.] [Maximum mark: 16]

Let $y = \dfrac{\ln x}{x^4}$ for $x > 0$.

\medskip

\textbf{(a)} Show that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}
= \dfrac{1 - 4\ln x}{x^5}$. \hfill [3]

\textbf{Solution:}

Using the quotient rule with $u = \ln x$, $v = x^4$:

$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}
= \dfrac{\frac{1}{x} \cdot x^4 - \ln x \cdot 4x^3}{(x^4)^2}$ \hfill \textbf{(M1)}

$= \dfrac{x^3 - 4x^3 \ln x}{x^8}$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

$= \dfrac{x^3(1 - 4\ln x)}{x^8}
= \dfrac{1 - 4\ln x}{x^5}$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)(AG)}

\newpage

\textbf{(b)} The graph of $f$ has a horizontal tangent at point $P$.
Find the coordinates of $P$. \hfill [5]

\textbf{Solution:}

Horizontal tangent: $f'(x) = 0$ \hfill \textbf{(M1)}

$\dfrac{1 - 4\ln x}{x^5} = 0$

Since $x^5 > 0$ for $x > 0$: \quad $1 - 4\ln x = 0$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

$\ln x = \dfrac{1}{4} \implies x = e^{1/4}$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

Substitute into $f(x)$: \hfill \textbf{(M1)}

$y = \dfrac{\ln(e^{1/4})}{(e^{1/4})^4}
= \dfrac{\frac{1}{4}}{e} = \dfrac{1}{4e}$

$P = \left(e^{1/4},\; \dfrac{1}{4e}\right)$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

\bigskip

\textbf{(c)} Given that $f''(x) = \dfrac{20\ln x - 9}{x^6}$,
show that $P$ is a local maximum point. \hfill [3]

\textbf{Solution:}

Substitute $x = e^{1/4}$ into $f''(x)$:
\quad $\ln(e^{1/4}) = \dfrac{1}{4}$ \hfill \textbf{(M1)}

$f''(e^{1/4}) = \dfrac{20 \cdot \frac{1}{4} - 9}{(e^{1/4})^6}
= \dfrac{5 - 9}{e^{3/2}} = \dfrac{-4}{e^{3/2}}$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}

Since $\dfrac{-4}{e^{3/2}} < 0$, $P$ is a local maximum point. \hfill \textbf{(R1)(AG)}

\bigskip

\textbf{(d)} Solve $f(x) > 0$ for $x > 0$. \hfill [2]

\textbf{Solution:}

$\dfrac{\ln x}{x^4} > 0$

Since $x^4 > 0$ for all $x > 0$, the sign depends on $\ln x$. \hfill \textbf{(R1)}

$\ln x > 0 \iff x > 1$

Answer: $x > 1$ \hfill \textbf{(A1)}
