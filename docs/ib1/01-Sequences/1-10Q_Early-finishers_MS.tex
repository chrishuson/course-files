\subsubsection*{1.10 Early Finishers: Sequences and Compound Interest --- Markscheme}

\emph{Extension set, scored separately from the quiz. Calculator allowed.
Total: 19 marks. Answers are exact or correct to three significant
figures.}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.3cm]

%--- Problem 1: Arithmetic sequence from two conditions; count of terms below 500; arithmetic terms forming a geometric sequence [11 marks] ---
\item[1.] [Maximum mark: 11]

$u_{3} + u_{8} = 48$, \ $u_{12} = 5\,u_{2}$.

\medskip

\textbf{(a)} Write each term as $u_{1} + (n - 1)d$: \\
$(u_{1} + 2d) + (u_{1} + 7d) = 48$ \hfill \textbf{M1} \\
$2u_{1} + 9d = 48$ \\
$u_{1} + 11d = 5(u_{1} + d)$ \hfill \textbf{M1} \\
$4u_{1} = 6d$ \\
$u_{1} = 1.5d$ \\
$2(1.5d) + 9d = 48$ \\
$12d = 48$ \\
$d = 4$ \hfill \textbf{A1} \\
$u_{1} = 1.5(4) = 6$ \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{One M1 for each condition written in terms of $u_{1}$ and $d$.
Accept solving the system $2u_{1} + 9d = 48$, $4u_{1} - 6d = 0$ on the
GDC. FT: award the final A1 for $u_{1}$ correct from the candidate's own
$d$.}

\medskip

\textbf{(b)} $6 + 4(n - 1) < 500$ \hfill \textbf{M1} \\
$4(n - 1) < 494$ \\
$n - 1 < 123.5$ \\
$n < 124.5$ \hfill \textbf{A1} \\
$124$ terms \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{Accept an equation in place of the inequality, or a table search
showing $u_{124} = 498$ and $u_{125} = 502$ (markscheme-spec §18). The
final A1 is for the count, $124$, not for $124.5$. FT from the
candidate's $u_{1}$ and $d$.}

\medskip

\textbf{(c)} The $1^{\text{st}}$, $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$
terms are $3$, \ $3 + d$, \ $3 + 3d$. \hfill \textbf{(M1)} \\
$\dfrac{3 + d}{3} = \dfrac{3 + 3d}{3 + d}$ \hfill \textbf{M1} \\
$(3 + d)^{2} = 3(3 + 3d)$ \\
$d^{2} - 3d = 0$ \\
$d \neq 0$, so $d = 3$ \hfill \textbf{A1}

The terms are $3,\; 6,\; 12$, so $r = 2$ \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{Second M1 for equal ratios, or the equivalent $(3 + d)^{2} = 3(3 +
3d)$. Award A0 for $d = 3$ if $d = 0$ is given as a second answer and not
rejected. FT: award the final A1 for $r$ correct from the candidate's own
$d$. $d = 0$ also solves the equation (the constant sequence $3, 3, 3$),
which is why the question excludes it.}

\newpage

%--- Problem 2: Compound interest -- doubling time; unknown rate; first year one investment overtakes another [8 marks] ---
\item[2.] [Maximum mark: 8]

Sofia: $PV = 5000$, $4.8\%$ compounded monthly. \ Luca: $PV = 6000$,
$r\%$ compounded quarterly, $FV = 9000$ after $10$ years.

\medskip

\textbf{(a)} $FV = PV\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$, with
$k = 12$:

$5000\left(1 + \dfrac{4.8}{100(12)}\right)^{12n} > 10\,000$ \hfill
\textbf{M1}

\begin{tabular}{cc}
$n$ (years) & Value \\
\hline
$14$ & \$$9777.63$ \\
$15$ & \$$10\,257.42$ \\
\end{tabular}
\hfill \textbf{A1}

$15$ years \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{M1 for the substituted expression, or the TVM entries $I\% = 4.8$,
$PV = -5000$, $PMT = 0$, $FV = 10\,000$, $P/Y = C/Y = 12$. First A1 for
evidence that $14$ years is not enough: the $14$-year value, or
$N = 173.6$ months $= 14.47$ years. A bare ``$15$'' with no working earns M0A0A1.}

\medskip

\textbf{(b)} $6000\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100(4)}\right)^{4(10)} = 9000$
\hfill \textbf{M1}

$\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{400}\right)^{40} = 1.5$

$r = 400\left(1.5^{1/40} - 1\right)$

$r = 4.08 \quad (4.07527\ldots)$ \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{A1 for $4.08$ (3 s.f.) or the calculator display with an ellipsis
($4.07527\ldots$); $4.075$ or $4.0753$ with no ellipsis earns A0. Accept the TVM solver: $N = 40$,
$PV = -6000$, $PMT = 0$, $FV = 9000$, $P/Y = C/Y = 4$, solve for
$I\%$.}

\medskip

\textbf{(c)} Sofia: $S_{n} = 5000\left(1 + \dfrac{4.8}{100(12)}\right)^{12n}$
\qquad Luca: $L_{n} = 6000\left(1 + \dfrac{4.0752\ldots}{100(4)}\right)^{4n}$
\hfill \textbf{M1}

\begin{tabular}{ccc}
$n$ (years) & Sofia & Luca \\
\hline
$24$ & \$$15\,786.26$ & \$$15\,877.07$ \\
$25$ & \$$16\,560.90$ & \$$16\,534.06$ \\
\end{tabular}
\hfill \textbf{A1}

Year $25$ \hfill \textbf{A1}

\emph{M1 for both expressions, or a GDC table of both. First A1 for the
adjacent-year comparison: Luca ahead at year $24$, Sofia ahead at year
$25$. Accept values from $r = 4.08$ rounded (Luca \$$15\,894.92$ and
\$$16\,553.42$); the answer is still year $25$. FT from the candidate's
$r$ in (b). Sofia starts with less but earns the higher effective rate
($4.91\%$ a year against $4.14\%$), so she catches up slowly.}

\end{enumerate}
